How it works

  • Finding the paper: if a reference has a DOI, that DOI is looked up directly. Otherwise the reference text goes to Crossref's bibliographic search and the top three results are compared.
  • Checking every part: the reference is then compared with the Crossref record piece by piece.
    • Authors: every surname, the author order, and each author's first initial. Authors left out are flagged unless the reference uses “et al.”
    • Title: word by word, reporting missing and extra words. Case and punctuation are ignored.
    • Journal: the full name or a standard abbreviation such as “N Engl J Med”.
    • Year, volume, issue and pages: exact values. The year may be the online or the print year. Shortened page ranges such as 1716-20 are expanded.
    • DOI: whether it is in Crossref and points to this paper.
  • Results: Match means every part agrees. Errors found means the paper exists but some details are wrong, and the Crossref values are shown so you can fix them. Likely means a close but uncertain match. Unverified means Crossref returned nothing close. DOI mismatch means the DOI belongs to a different paper.
  • A ! marks a part that Crossref has but the reference leaves out, such as a missing issue number. It is not counted as an error.
  • Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is stored on a server. Requests go only to api.crossref.org.
  • Crossref covers most journal articles and many books, but not everything. An Unverified result means the reference could not be found, not that it is wrong. Check it by hand.
